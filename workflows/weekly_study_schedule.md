# Weekly Study Schedule

## Purpose

This document defines the standard weekly study structure for my
long-term development toward software engineering and quantitative
development.

The schedule separates:

- formal curriculum hours;
- homework and deliberate practice;
- physical training;
- rest and recovery.

The curriculum is evaluated in four-week blocks and adjusted based on
progress, workload, and long-term goals.

## Homework Schedule

The evening study period is intended primarily for processing material
from the formal curriculum.

It should not normally be used to introduce large amounts of unrelated
new material.

## Homework Priorities

Evening work should generally follow this order:

1. Resolve significant gaps or unfinished work.
2. Complete exercises assigned during lessons.
3. Practice recently learned material.
4. Retrieve important concepts from memory.
5. Prepare for an upcoming lesson.
6. Review material that needs additional repetition.

Homework does not replace formal curriculum hours.

For example, two hours of scheduled Linear Algebra during the formal
curriculum remain two curriculum hours even if additional Linear
Algebra exercises are completed in the evening.

---

## Study Method

For technical subjects, prefer active work over passive consumption.

A typical learning cycle is:

1. Study the concept.
2. Attempt the problem independently.
3. Run, calculate, or test the solution.
4. Identify errors.
5. Correct the solution.
6. Repeat without relying on the example.
7. Record important reusable knowledge where appropriate.

For programming:

> Code first. Documentation supports the skill; it does not replace it.

For mathematics:

> Read → retrieve → calculate → check → explain.

Videos should preferably be combined with active processing rather than
watched continuously.

For example:

> Video → notes → textbook → exercises.

---

## Training

Physical training takes place outside the formal curriculum.

On days with evening homework, training should normally be completed
before 20:30.

The purpose of this separation is to prevent study from consistently
displacing physical training and recovery.

## Weekly Review

At the end of each study week, review:

1. How many scheduled hours were actually completed?
2. Which subjects progressed as expected?
3. Which subjects require additional practice?
4. Where was the cognitive workload too high?
5. Which homework blocks were effective?
6. Did studying interfere with training or recovery?
7. What should change next week?

---

## Four-Week Review

At the end of each four-week curriculum block:

- assess progress for each subject;
- evaluate the workload;
- review completed outputs;
- identify weak areas;
- adjust weekly hours where necessary;
- determine whether new subjects should be introduced;
- create the next schedule version.

Schedule versions should be updated deliberately:
```text
v01 → initial schedule
v02 → first structural revision
v03 → subsequent structural revision
```
Small day-to-day changes do not require a new version.

---

## Core Principles
1. Python remains the primary programming subject during Block 1.
2. Formal curriculum hours and homework are separate.
3. Homework is primarily for retrieval, exercises, and preparation.
4. Active practice takes priority over passive study.
5. Documentation should support rather than replace programming.
6. Physical training remains outside formal study hours.
7. Study normally ends at 22:30.
8. 22:30–00:00 is reserved for relaxation and personal time.
9. The curriculum is evaluated every four weeks.
10. Long-term expansion should be gradual and evidence-based.
